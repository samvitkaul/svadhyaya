# Quadratic norms: Pell equations and geometry

For square-free positive $D$, the field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ has conjugation $a+b\sqrt D\mapsto a-b\sqrt D$ and norm

$$N(a+b\sqrt D)=a^2-Db^2.$$

The norm-one equation

$$a^2-Db^2=1$$

is a hyperbola in real coordinates. Integral solutions are Pell-type equations, generating arithmetic structure through multiplication of units.

For $\mathbb Q(i)$, the corresponding norm $a^2+b^2$ has circular norm-one level sets. These contrasting shapes follow from the signatures of quadratic forms; they are **not by themselves** complete classifications of Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry.
